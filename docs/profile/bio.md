## 300 words

300 words.

```
Pearse D'Arcy is a software engineer at MTS – The Wetlab Company, a wetlab-education company based in Innsbruck. He is the sole engineer on the platforms it runs on, and he builds them with a team of AI agents whose work he reviews before it merges.

The longest-running of those platforms is wetlabs.app, a surgical-training video platform. A camera on each wetlab station records the surgeon; a supervised phase model, trained on the company's own footage and judged against a locked test set, splits each procedure into chapters; and every surgeon gets a personal video library. It runs at live congresses and deploys on every merge. The general vision-language model stays as an opt-in.

MTS Hub is the operations system of record: congresses, courses, contacts, bookings, packing, staffing, certificates and communications on one model. It replaced spreadsheets, a FileMaker database and two earlier tools he had built, and it carries more than 6000 automated tests, 22 architecture decision records and a human-approval gate before any email leaves the system.

Behind both sits an observability stack on OpenTelemetry, OpenObserve and GlitchTip, with an AI agent that reads each alert and posts a first diagnosis. He also built the agentic engineering workflow that makes the rest possible: one planning session, cheaper delegate agents routed by issue size, git worktree isolation enforced by hooks, and adversarial review in which one model argues against every pull request.

Before software, he spent ten years racing downhill skateboards: 2nd overall in the 2019 IDF European rankings (European vice champion) and BDSL (British Downhill Skateboard League) champion. Building Skatedownhills, an events calendar for the sport, brought him back to code. He taught himself, took a full-stack diploma in 2024 and worked freelance on Django, Wagtail, WordPress and TYPO3 sites. He is from Dublin and writes at pearse.dev.
```
